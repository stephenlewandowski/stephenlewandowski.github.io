Dates follow the selected calendar. Each point is a daily pair averaged over at least 18 matching hours. Move over or select a point to inspect its date and values. Keyboard: tab into the plot, then use the left and right arrows to move between days.

Compare regression models

A straight line summarizes one direction of association. A quadratic can bend. The within-month model asks whether warmer or cooler days also had different PM₂.₅ within the same month, reducing the influence of the annual cycle.

What the selected model leaves unexplained

Residuals are observed PM₂.₅ minus fitted PM₂.₅. Runs of positive or negative values can show patterns that a single curve misses.

These fits describe associations between environmental records. The data contain no health outcomes, individual exposures or military cohort records, so the tool cannot estimate the health effects of simultaneous pollution and thermal stress. Successive days can be correlated, and the app does not present ordinary independent-day p-values or confidence bands.

Compare city-specific associations

Apply the same measure, calendar and date filters to each city. Each row is fitted separately; there is no pooled line that mixes differences among cities with changes within them.

Seoul uses the chosen PM₂.₅ record above; its weather grid cell stays the same for all districts. Other city labels refer to one pollution monitoring location. Coverage and the spatial match differ among cities.

How often did the conditions occur together?

Choose daily thresholds to count pollution alongside higher or lower thermal values. These counts use the eligible paired days in the selection.

Counts include equality and describe co-occurrence. For a cold-weather comparison, choose “at or below” and a lower temperature or UTCI threshold. A low relative-humidity threshold with the winter filter explores dry winter days. These are comparison settings, not health-risk boundaries or a test of physiological interaction. Matching-hour means and a single year do not establish PM₂.₅ regulatory attainment.

Data and methods

Pairing daily records

The project reuses the measured hourly PM₂.₅ from the air-quality comparison and archived ERA5 weather from the heat-stress comparison. Hours are joined by UTC timestamp. Each daily pair uses identical valid hours for its pollution and thermal means, with at least 18 matches in a complete calendar day. The file’s actual start time is respected, including Seoul’s district records.

Calendar and coverage

Local standard time uses fixed offsets: Seoul and Pyeongtaek UTC+9, Beijing UTC+8, London GMT and Los Angeles PST. Daylight saving time is not applied. Partial boundary days and days with insufficient matching hours are excluded. Date and season filters select daily dates in the chosen calendar. Missing values are not filled; reported pollution zeros remain zero.

What the records represent

Pollution monitors and weather grid cells describe different spatial areas. Seoul’s network mean requires at least 20 of 25 district monitors per hour, and individual districts are selectable. A district’s weather remains the city’s nearest ERA5 grid cell. Pyeongtaek has an August outage and long reported-zero runs without per-reading quality flags; those limitations carry into its fits.

UTCI and ERA5-HEAT

This release uses the existing UTCI scenario with mean radiant temperature equal to air temperature. It does not use published ERA5-HEAT values. ERA5-HEAT provides hourly UTCI and radiant temperature for 2024, but its documented download route requires a CDS account, token and accepted dataset terms. Its radiation-aware UTCI would be a useful next data source.

WBGT and averaging

WBGT uses the Liljegren model, with exposed-outdoor and no-shortwave scenarios. The heat project’s evaporation-property filter leaves some hours unavailable. Paired days can therefore represent a smaller, warmer subset of the year. Each heat index is calculated hourly before daily averaging; coverage is specific to the selected measure.

Interpreting regression

Linear and quadratic ordinary least squares describe the selected daily pairs. The month-adjusted slope uses deviations from each month’s paired-day means. Its within-month R² measures variation explained after removing monthly means. It is not directly comparable to the raw-data R². See NIST on seasonality and autocorrelation.

The motivation is to explore pollution alongside both heat and cold. This page examines exposure patterns and meteorological associations. Testing whether temperature modifies pollution’s effects on health requires outcome data, a suitable study design and additional control for confounding.
