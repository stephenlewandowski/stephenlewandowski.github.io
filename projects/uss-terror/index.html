USS Terror (CM-5): Pacific deployment, 1943–1946

An evidence-aware chronology and spatial reconstruction that distinguishes documented locations, modeled connections, disputed evidence, and uncertainty.

Status: Live on Posit Connect Cloud

01 · Overview

A documented Pacific deployment.

The project reconstructs USS Terror's wartime and immediate postwar deployment as a 61-event chronology, 57 route legs, an interactive Shiny application, a reproducible spatial pipeline, QGIS exchange files, validation reports, and a publication map series.

The project combines the deployment chronology and modern-context foundation with a focused Kerama Retto evidence set and a candidate-review mode that keeps research candidates separate from canonical records.

02 · Evidence model

Sources and uncertainty stay visible.

Dates may be exact, estimated, or ranges. Coordinates retain their basis—such as a port, anchorage, or island centroid—and their position uncertainty in nautical miles. Low-confidence, excluded, disputed, and overlapping records are flagged for review rather than silently repaired.

Connecting lines are geodesic interpolations between documented or modeled endpoints. They are not exact ship tracks, and the project does not invent daily positions between widely spaced records.

03 · Shiny application

One interface for maps, records, and review.

The R/Shiny application brings together a Pacific Theater map, timeline, deployment tables, route metrics, grouped research pages, and historical mine-warfare evidence review. Shared filters keep the map, timeline, tables, counts, and selections synchronized.

OpenStreetMap is the normal online base. A generated Natural Earth boundary-and-label layer supports local operation without map tiles, while optional modern seamark and bathymetric layers are clearly separated from wartime evidence.

04 · Methods & outputs

Reproducible from data to map.

Canonical spatial data use WGS84 (EPSG:4326). Great-circle connections are generated locally and split at the antimeridian so Pacific routes do not wrap across Eurasia or the Atlantic. Position-uncertainty areas are geodesic circles rather than planar latitude-longitude buffers.

Outputs include processed event, route, and location tables; GeoPackages for QGIS; conflict and source inventories; validation reports; and map products covering the full deployment, Iwo Jima and Okinawa, the kamikaze attack and repair period, postwar operations, and confidence and uncertainty.

05 · Mine-warfare research

Candidates remain separate from canonical data.

The canonical mine-warfare tables remain deliberately empty. The evidence review resolved ten candidate decisions as accepted and retained one unsupported USS Terror event-level relationship as rejected. The app exposes canonical, accepted-candidate, and rejected-audit views without automatically promoting any candidate.

This is an incomplete historical reconstruction. It is not current hazard information and must not be used for navigation, route planning, diving, fishing, salvage, ordnance clearance, or safety decisions.

06 · Next research step

Verify sources before adding records.

The next step is to retrieve the original National Archives LSM(R)-194 deck-log sheet and the complete Mine Squadron Four report, verify the accepted reproductions, and design a controlled import for accepted candidates only.
