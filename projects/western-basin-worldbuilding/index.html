Science and imagination

Real constraints. Possible lives.

Western Basin Worldbuilding starts with real regional geography and documented relationships among water, ecology, agriculture, energy, freight and public infrastructure. The Atlas translates those relationships into places: a canopy joined to an old brick wall, a drainage gate on a working farm, a repair berth in an industrial hall.

Studies and connection charts explain the relationships. Architectural sketches show imagined devices and access. The future buildings, characters and institutions are design concepts rather than forecasts.

The Waterville explorer presents a retained USGS daily river-flow record. Model Lab runs a separate scientific track: synthetic water and nutrient accounting cases with a local R/Shiny explorer. Those cases test routing and assumptions; they do not establish measured nutrient loads or calibrated future predictions.

The original sketchbook brings early buildings, working machines and character designs into view. Frontier Arc’s rural maker ideas now sit within Black Swamp Country; the original sheets keep their earlier labels.

A growing world, open to exploration.

Illustrated regions, ordinary-life encounters and small browser games give this world several ways in. The parallel scientific work can deepen as measured inputs mature.
