Teaching / decision brief · Drinking-water resilience

What can be concluded now?

A short, evidence-bounded exercise using three public records to separate documented facts, bounded synthesis, and evidence still needed.

Status: Public teaching material · evidence snapshot reviewed 17 August 2026
Return to project overview

AudienceWater-sector operators and leaders, public-health and emergency-management professionals, researchers, and advanced students

Time20–30 minutes

Primary taskClassify evidence and identify the next checks

FallbackPrintable page; no live application required

01 · Three case examples

Different records, different evidence limits.

Read the cases as deliberately different public records. They support related questions, but they are not a common forensic dataset.

Case 1 · Plymouth, Minnesota

Manual continuity during a communications outage

Plymouth reported a communications outage affecting two water towers and multiple lift stations connected through cellular communications after a suspected cyberattack. Crews continued manual procedures; the city reported no impact to water levels or water quality, later restored communications and normal operations, and continued monitoring.

Source: City of Plymouth municipal update

Do not infer: Independent forensics, a device-model match, or sector-wide effectiveness.

Case 2 · Minot, North Dakota

SCADA ransomware with manual monitoring

City officials said ransomware was detected on 14 March 2026 on a server supporting the Minot Water Treatment Plant’s SCADA system. Operators disconnected the affected server and used more frequent, on-location gauge readings for about 16 hours while a replacement server was prepared; officials reported that the plant remained operational and the water supply stayed safe.

Source: City account reported by StateScoop

Do not infer: The identity of the actor, a confirmed effect on water quality, or that manual monitoring alone proves resilience.

Case 3 · Aliquippa, Pennsylvania

PLC compromise at a pressure-control booster station

In November 2023, the Municipal Water Authority of Aliquippa reported that an Iranian-backed group compromised a PLC at a remote booster station used to monitor and regulate water pressure. The device was disabled and operated manually; the authority reported no known risk to drinking water or water supply.

Source: WaterISAC incident advisory

Do not infer: Broader compromise, contamination, attribution beyond the advisory’s account, or sector-wide risk from one device.

Scope rule: These are differently scoped public records, not a directly comparable dataset. Plymouth illustrates manual continuity and restoration; Minot illustrates SCADA ransomware and manual monitoring; Aliquippa illustrates a PLC compromise at a pressure-control station. Do not collapse them into one incident account or infer water-quality effects beyond the cited public statements.

02 · Decision prompt

Advise a leader after a reported cyber disruption.

Take the role of a utility leader, public-health official, emergency manager, or commander. Use the three cases to make a bounded assessment, not a confident story.

  1. What can be concluded now? Name the record and its scope.
  2. What cannot be concluded? Identify at least one inference that would overreach the evidence.
  3. What evidence is needed next? Separate operational status, water-safety verification, recovery checks, and communication needs.
  4. What should be communicated now? State what is known, what is being checked, and what has not been confirmed.

03 · Response frame

Write the answer in four columns of thought.

QuestionBounded response
What is documented?Name the source, date, record type, observed effect, and geographic or facility scope.
What is not established?State the most important limit: attribution, contamination, comparability, implementation, or recovery assurance.
What evidence is needed next?Identify checks for control and process status, water quality and public-health protection, recovery, and communication.
What should be communicated now?Report what is known, what is under review, and what has not been confirmed; avoid implying more than the record supports.

Debrief

  • Cyber access is not the same as a physical-process consequence.
  • Restored communications are not, by themselves, proof of water-safety assurance.
  • A municipal continuity report is not sector-wide evidence.
  • Justified confidence requires explicit verification, monitoring, and communication—not a label or score.
