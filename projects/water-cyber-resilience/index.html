01 · Overview

Separate claims require separate evidence.

The exhibit follows a reported path from cyber access and loss of view or control through operational effects, continuity, water-safety assurance, recovery verification, and justified confidence.

Its central distinction is simple: an intrusion, automation loss, physical consequence, manual continuity, safe-water assurance, recovery, and confidence are not one claim. Each stage has a different evidence requirement.

The release is intended for water-sector operators and leaders, public-health and emergency-management professionals, infrastructure-resilience researchers, and advanced students.

04 · Source boundary

Primary government sources only.

The release uses four registered primary government sources: a federal FBI/EPA incident notice, EPA enforcement guidance, a Minnesota response notice, and a Plymouth municipal update. Evidence snapshot date: 13 August 2026.

  • FBI/EPA federal incident notice — aggregate access, reported operational effects, and recovery recommendations.
  • EPA enforcement alert — domestic risk-and-resilience planning and cybersecurity baseline.
  • Minnesota IT Services response notice — statewide targeting and coordinated response context.
  • City of Plymouth municipal update — one municipal continuity, water-status, restoration, and monitoring report.

The Plymouth page was directly reviewed on 10 August 2026; official-domain retrieval reconfirmed its title, post date, and displayed claims on 13 August, while automated direct retrieval returned HTTP 403. That known currentness limitation is retained in the release record.

Interpretation limits

This exhibit does not attribute an actor, report confirmed contamination, diagnose the water sector, evaluate a specific utility's cybersecurity, or claim that manual operations are universally safe or sustainable. It keeps an aggregated federal pattern separate from one municipal report and treats justified confidence as an evidence question, not a measured result.
