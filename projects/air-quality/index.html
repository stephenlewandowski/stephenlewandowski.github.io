How these statistics are calculated

The annual component gives each of the four quarterly means equal weight. The daily 98th percentile is a ranked daily value: the eighth-highest when 351–366 days are valid, or the seventh-highest with 301–350. It is not an interpolated percentile. Only a completed three-year design value would receive EPA’s final rounding.

Hourly inputs and daily means are truncated to one decimal place for this table. A day normally requires 18 hours. A day with fewer hours is retained only if its sum divided by 24 reaches 35.5 µg/m³; that conservative lower-bound mean is used and flagged below. This exception is not the threshold for counting days above 35.

Days run midnight to midnight in fixed local standard time: Seoul UTC+9, Beijing UTC+8, London GMT throughout the year. Full local-year Seoul observations are available here; missing boundary hours in the other records remain missing. Source hour labels are preserved, including Seoul’s unresolved start/end convention.

Primary standards protect public health; secondary standards protect public welfare. Levels and calculation rules checked 19 October 2026: EPA NAAQS table · 40 CFR part 50, Appendix N.

“Above” means strictly greater than the chosen concentration. Calendar-day means need at least 18 hours. For shorter date selections, only whole calendar days contained in the selected UTC period are counted; one UTC date may contain no complete local day. Local-time counts stay within the local calendar year 2024. Rolling windows need 75% of their hours; their end hour must fall within the selected dates and they may use earlier hours. Overlapping windows are not separate pollution events. Missing hours break consecutive runs.

There is no federal 1-hour or 8-hour PM₂.₅ standard. A count of hourly values above 35, or of trailing 24-hour means above 35, is an exploratory comparison. The U.S. daily standard uses local calendar days and a three-year percentile statistic.

Data and methods

What a city label means

Seoul defaults to an equal-weight average of its 25 district monitoring stations, calculated only when at least 20 report a valid value for that hour. It describes that monitoring network; it is not weighted by population. The selector also offers each district monitor.

Other records identify a single monitoring location in each city. Their location and surroundings affect the comparison. The source notes below name each station. Network averages should not be treated as site-level regulatory records.

Averaging and missing hours

A daily mean requires at least 18 of 24 hourly values. Rolling means require a complete time window and at least 75% of its values: six of eight hours, or eighteen of twenty-four. Calculations use the available values within an eligible window.

Missing values remain gaps. No model estimates or interpolated concentrations are added. Zero readings remain zero. The chart starts a new line segment after a missing plotted value.

Dates and units

The time series uses the UTC calendar year 2024: 8,784 hours, including 29 February. Its daily means use UTC days. Threshold counts can use UTC or local standard-time days; the U.S. standards table always uses local standard time. PM₂.₅ is measured in µg/m³, not ppm.

Seoul’s source describes hourly means without specifying whether its hour labels mark the start or end of the interval. Those labels are preserved. Nine unavailable UTC hours at the end of the year remain missing. The standards table uses the full Korean calendar-year file, including its first nine hours before UTC 2024.

Using the comparison

Start with Seoul and London, then add another station. Compare a whole year with a single month, or switch between hourly and daily values. An average can reduce the appearance of a brief peak without changing the underlying measurements.

Differences also reflect geography, monitor siting and data capture. This figure is a teaching and historical comparison, not a current air-quality forecast.
