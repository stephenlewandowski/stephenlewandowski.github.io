Data and methods

What a city label means

Seoul uses an equal-weight average of its 25 district monitoring stations, calculated only when at least 20 report a valid value for that hour. It describes that monitoring network; it is not weighted by population.

Other records identify a single monitoring location in each city. Their location and surroundings affect the comparison. The source notes below name each station.

Averaging and missing hours

A daily mean requires at least 18 of 24 hourly values. Rolling means require a complete time window and at least 75% of its values: six of eight hours, or eighteen of twenty-four. Calculations use the available values within an eligible window.

Missing values remain gaps. No model estimates or interpolated concentrations are added. Zero readings remain zero. The chart starts a new line segment after a missing plotted value.

Dates and units

The common grid is the UTC calendar year 2024: 8,784 hours, including 29 February. Daily means use UTC days so all records share the same boundaries. A trailing average includes the displayed hour and preceding hours.

Seoul’s source describes hourly means without specifying whether its hour labels mark the start or end of the interval. Those labels are preserved. Nine unavailable UTC hours at the end of the year remain missing. UTC daily means may differ from the local-day means used by an official reporting agency.

Using the comparison

Start with Seoul and London, then add another station. Compare a whole year with a single month, or switch between hourly and daily values. An average can reduce the appearance of a brief peak without changing the underlying measurements.

Differences also reflect geography, monitor siting and data capture. This figure is a teaching and historical comparison, not a current air-quality forecast.
