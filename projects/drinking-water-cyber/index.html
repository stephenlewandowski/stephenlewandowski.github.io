01 · Overview

Separate the claims that are often collapsed.

A cyber incident is not a single outcome. Access, loss of view or control, physical-process effects, service continuity, water-safety assurance, recovery verification, and justified confidence are different claims with different evidence requirements.

The project keeps federal aggregate reporting, dated municipal accounts, governance guidance, operational-technology frameworks, bounded synthesis, and open analytical questions visibly distinct. It does not attribute an actor, report confirmed contamination, diagnose the water sector, or treat one municipality as representative of all systems.

02 · Project outline

A consequence path with parallel evidence questions.

  1. Reported cyber access. What access or configuration change is documented?
  2. Loss of view or control. What operator capability was lost?
  3. Operational outcome. Did the reported loss produce degradation, or did operators sustain service manually?
  4. Safety assurance. What evidence supports conclusions about water levels, water quality, and service continuity?
  5. Recovery verification. What was restored, checked, monitored, and communicated?
  6. Justified confidence. What evidence would make confidence warranted rather than assumed?

The branches are analytical, not a universal incident chronology. The complete source-linked table remains the static fallback when the optional evidence lens is unavailable.

03 · Evidence boundary

Cases provide contrasts, not a pooled dataset.

The current exhibit includes the Plymouth manual-continuity account, the Minot SCADA-ransomware case, and the Aliquippa PLC-compromise case as distinct public examples. Their dates, reporting purposes, evidence quality, and operational detail differ; the project does not merge them into one forensic narrative.

Manual operation is treated as a capability whose safety and sustainability depend on context. Restoration of communications or automation is not itself proof of safe water, reliable service, or justified confidence.

What the project does not claim

It does not establish contamination, identify every affected facility, compare utilities by cybersecurity performance, or convert incident reports into an operational threat score. It asks what can be concluded now, what cannot, and what evidence is needed next.
