Compare the measures

Air temperature, UTCI and WBGT all use degrees Celsius, but their scales and assumptions differ. Relative humidity uses percent. A value of 30 does not mean the same heat stress in each measure.

Understanding heat risk

Hot conditions are one part of a heat-risk assessment. Who encounters them, their susceptibility and the protection available also matter.

Four questions that need different evidence
QuestionExampleEvidence needed
HazardHow hot was it, and for how long?The weather estimates and calculated heat indices shown above describe environmental conditions.
ExposureWho encountered those conditions?Location, activity and time spent in the conditions are needed to estimate personal exposure.
VulnerabilityWho is more susceptible, and what protection is available?Local information about health, housing, working conditions and access to cooling.
Health outcomesWhat illness or harm occurred?Health surveillance and a study design suited to the question.

Xuewen Lei and colleagues' 2026 study, Social vulnerability amplifies global inequality in urban heat risk, in Communications Earth & Environment, examined 1,029 cities and how including social vulnerability changes a heat-risk comparison. Its 2000–2019 analysis uses different data and heatwave definitions from this page. The five-city records here cover 2023–2025; they support historical comparisons of conditions, rather than long-term climate trends or estimates of heat deaths.

Research discovery credit: Ho Woo Nam's DeBriefed, 16 October 2026, published by Carbon Brief. The original study is credited above; this explanation is a project synthesis.

Choosing heat data for a research question

The comparisons above describe historical environmental conditions. A study of health, outdoor work or an intervention also needs evidence matched to its population, location and time window.

Choices to check before reusing a heat estimate
ChoiceWhat to check
Heat metricEach index has its own inputs, assumptions and interpretation. Values and thresholds are not interchangeable across indices.
Time and placeA county daily summary, a city grid estimate and conditions during an outdoor shift represent different exposure windows and areas.
AggregationArea and population weighting answer different questions. Preserve the order of index calculation, spatial averaging and daily summaries.

Data and methods

Weather data

Hourly 2023–2025 ERA5 reanalysis is retrieved through Open-Meteo’s Historical Weather API. ERA5 combines observations with a weather model. Native nearest grid cells are selected, without elevation downscaling. A cell describes an area roughly 25 km across; it cannot resolve a shaded street, an indoor workplace or every neighborhood.

Weather data: Open-Meteo and Copernicus/ECMWF ERA5, redistributed under CC BY 4.0. Heat indices, rounding and aggregates are calculated for this project. Data attribution and licence.

Temperature and humidity

Air temperature and relative humidity are at 2 m above the model surface. Wind is supplied at 10 m. All cities use the same source and processing. Each archive spans a full UTC year: 8,760 hours in 2023 and 2025, and 8,784 in leap year 2024. It is a historical comparison, not a forecast.

UTCI scenario

The Universal Thermal Climate Index combines air temperature, humidity, wind and radiant temperature. This version sets mean radiant temperature equal to air temperature: no added radiant heat. It does not reproduce sun-exposed UTCI. The polynomial uses its published validity limits; wind below 0.5 m/s is set to 0.5 m/s and flagged in the source notes.

WBGT estimates

Wet-bulb globe temperature is calculated using the ECMWF thermofeel implementation of the Liljegren model. The outdoor estimate uses modeled instantaneous sunlight, pressure, humidity and wind. A second scenario removes shortwave radiation. Both retain the model’s surface and sky assumptions; neither is a workplace globe measurement.

This project omits WBGT when the mean of air and natural wet-bulb temperature falls outside the evaporation property fit’s 283–313 K range. This is a conservative project filter, not a validation boundary for the whole model. WBGT means therefore describe eligible hours, with coverage shown.

Averages and counts

Each index is calculated from hourly weather before any averaging. A heat index calculated from an annual mean temperature and humidity would give a different, misleading quantity. Daily summaries need at least 18 valid hours; trailing means need 75% of the hours in a complete window. The count of dates with any qualifying hour includes known occurrences on incomplete days. Missing and out-of-domain values remain gaps.

Interpretation

The thresholds compare historical conditions. They do not prescribe safe work or exercise, establish a regulatory exceedance, or account for a particular person’s health and activity. Sources: UTCI, National Weather Service WBGT guidance and the calculation notes below.
