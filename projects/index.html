Featured project

USS Terror (CM-5): Pacific deployment, 1943–1946

This family-history project began with a practical research gap: my grandfather served aboard USS Terror during this period, but I could not find a campaign-course map specific to the ship. It combines an evidence-aware reconstruction, a 61-event chronology, 57 route legs, an interactive R/Shiny application, reproducible spatial processing, validation reports, static maps, and QGIS exchange files. Documented facts, modeled connections, disputed evidence, and uncertainty remain visibly distinct.

Featured project · audited publication

Korean Peninsula Aedes suitability, 1975–2024.

A reproducible regional analysis connects modeled seasonal suitability, ambient population exposure, and reviewed ROK observations without treating them as interchangeable evidence.

The project compares fixed early, middle, and recent decades for Aedes aegypti and Aedes albopictus across 370 source-grid cells. It adds a fully reconciled LandScan population join, 54 reviewed ROK presence-only records, and six separate effort-aware pooled trap-index sites. The publication site provides audited maps, compact data products, transparent methods, and explicit limits on disease-risk or operational interpretation.

Source-linked exhibit · evidence lens

From cyber access to justified confidence.

A source-linked drinking-water cyber-resilience consequence path that keeps operational effects, safe-water assurance, recovery, and confidence as separate evidence questions.

The project asks what evidence connects a reported intrusion to operational effects, safe-water assurance, recovery, and justified confidence. It combines a consequence-path diagram, an accessible evidence-class table, and machine-readable CSV snapshots. Its evidence lens distinguishes documented incident records, municipal case examples from Plymouth, Minot, and Aliquippa, bounded synthesis, and analytical questions; the complete static table remains available as the fallback.
