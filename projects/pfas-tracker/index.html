01 · Overview

Trace requirements without assuming equivalence.

The tracker asks which source creates a requirement, who or what it reaches, how it is implemented or recognized overseas, and what evidence would show that an installation, component, or contractor has an obligation.

The immediate legal context is EPA's designation of PFOA and PFOS as CERCLA hazardous substances. On August 18, 2026, the D.C. Circuit denied petitions challenging that rule in Chamber of Commerce v. EPA, No. 24-1193. The tracker records that federal event as a source-linked development; it does not treat the court decision as a complete overseas or installation-level compliance answer.

02 · Project outline

Six linked layers of obligation.

  1. U.S. federal law and EPA action. Statutes, regulations, final rules, enforcement material, and court decisions.
  2. Overseas environmental governance. DoD policy and environmental requirements that frame overseas implementation.
  3. Country governing standards. Final Governing Standards and related country-specific provisions.
  4. SOFA and international instruments. Status, jurisdiction, and host-nation coordination context.
  5. Host-nation law and guidance. Initial South Korea, Japan, and Germany crosswalks.
  6. Installation, component, and contractor obligations. The operationally useful endpoint, tied to a specific provision and actor.

The layers preserve differences among legally binding requirements, policy, guidance, standards, recommendations, and analytical inferences.

03 · Current work

Build outward from the federal source base.

  • U.S. federal expansion: complete the federal rule, release-reporting, liability, drinking-water, cleanup, and agency-guidance source set.
  • Korea: perform the provision-level EGS crosswalk and identify where SOFA, host-nation requirements, and installation practice intersect.
  • Japan: add the corresponding country crosswalk while keeping legal status and guidance distinct.
  • Germany: retain the initial jurisdiction in the architecture for later source verification and comparison.

Current status is deliberately a build status, not a conclusion that overseas requirements are equivalent, weaker, or more protective than U.S. requirements.

Interpretation boundary

A federal designation, a court decision, a host-nation rule, an EGS provision, an installation practice, and a contractor obligation are not interchangeable. Each claim needs its own provision, source, date, scope, actor, and implementation evidence.
