Teaching portfolio

Applied evidence, visible reasoning, responsible judgment.

I teach environmental and occupational health as a field in which scientific evidence, engineering methods, public-health practice, regulation, and professional judgment meet.

Teaching philosophy

From preparation to defensible decisions.

My teaching begins with bounded preparation and moves quickly toward application. Cases ask learners to define a problem, select relevant evidence, state assumptions, compare feasible alternatives, and communicate a recommendation within its regulatory, ethical, and practical context.

Participation should make reasoning visible through discussion, calculations, concise writing, data interpretation, small-group analysis, presentations, or a defended recommendation. Assessment should test those capabilities rather than reward recall by default.

Read the full teaching philosophy

Evidence and uncertainty are central subject matter

Students need to distinguish evidence from assumption, data from interpretation, association from causation, requirements from recommendations, and numerical precision from genuine certainty. Environmental-health decisions often must be made before uncertainty disappears; learners should identify limitations and explain why a recommendation remains defensible.

Authentic tools should clarify, not obscure

Environmental measurements, surveillance indicators, GIS, exposure methods, hydrologic calculations, risk-assessment frameworks, technical standards, and regulatory data are useful when learners understand what each method can establish and where its result stops being reliable.

The goal is responsible professional judgment

I want students to leave able to make a defensible decision and revise it when better evidence becomes available. That requires technical skill, clarity about limits, attention to affected populations, and honest communication.
