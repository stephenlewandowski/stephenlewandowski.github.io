Drinking Water Treatment

A source-aware teaching resource that connects treatment decisions with regulatory status, professional guidance, proposed changes, and the limits of compliance datasets.

Open technical reportRead lecture notes
AudienceGraduate and professional learners
Teaching moveClassify evidence, then recommend
Verified throughAugust 9, 2026

Instructional design

Authority and status come before application.

Learners classify each claim as regulation, proposal, guidance, recommendation, assessment, or dataset; identify its jurisdiction and effective status; and apply it only within that scope.

Core content

  • Water Safety Plans as source-to-consumer risk management.
  • National primary drinking-water requirements and enforceability.
  • PFAS final requirements kept distinct from pending proposals.
  • Treatment technologies selected for a defined application.
  • SDWIS and ECHO documentation, refresh timing, and field interpretation.

Application sequence

  1. Compare three source statements and explain the authority each one carries.
  2. Frame a bounded treatment decision, stating facts, assumptions, and evidence gaps.
  3. Write a two-paragraph recommendation that separates current requirements, proposed changes, guidance, and uncertainty.

Professional reasoning

The resource treats currentness as a dated claim. It asks learners to show why a technically plausible conclusion can still be unusable when its authority, jurisdiction, or date semantics are wrong.

The report is educational and does not replace current regulatory review, site-specific engineering analysis, or utility decision making.

Currentness notice

Pending proposals are not final requirements.

The source baseline was verified on August 9, 2026. Regulatory status should be rechecked before operational use, including PFAS implementation timing and the status of pending proposals.
