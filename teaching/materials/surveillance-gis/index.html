Surveillance and GIS for Public-Health Decisions

A synthetic case in which learners must distinguish observation, interpretation, causal claim, and action before turning reported events into a geographic recommendation.

Decision case

A map is not an automatic causal conclusion.

A fictional regional health office receives four weeks of reports from six synthetic service areas. Teams decide what to verify first, where to consider investigation or outreach, and what geographic detail can be shared publicly.

Reasoning sequence

  • Define the public-health decision and the surveillance purpose.
  • Audit timeliness, completeness, representativeness, and reporting changes.
  • Align numerator, denominator, geography, and period before calculating rates.
  • Choose mapping units and classifications that match the decision.
  • Treat suppression as unavailable—not zero—and protect geographic privacy.
  • State what the pattern shows, does not establish, and supports doing next.

Module format

The 25-page, low-density deck uses a fictional regional health office and six synthetic service areas to connect surveillance reasoning with GIS choices.

All place names, values, and the classroom suppression rule are synthetic. They must not be cited as evidence about a real community.

Currentness and adaptation

Operational rules and data vintages require a fresh check.

Before adaptation, verify disease-specific reporting rules, service documentation, geographic vintages, denominators, and current privacy policies. Preserve the distinction between suppressed and zero.
