Environmental Health Risk Assessment

A complete sample course built around transparent problem formulation, exposure and dose-response evidence, uncertainty, reproducibility, and communication for decision support.

Open syllabus PDFRead web syllabus
AudienceGraduate students and professionals
Structure14 weeks · 8 objectives · 5 assessments
FormatComplete 14-week course design

Design premise

Risk assessment is scientific input to a decision—not the decision itself.

The syllabus develops the full reasoning sequence from problem formulation through risk characterization, while keeping risk-management choices visible and separate. Learners must distinguish evidence from assumption, variability from uncertainty, and requirements from professional guidance.

What the course asks learners to do

  • Build and defend source–pathway–receptor conceptual models.
  • Evaluate hazard, dose-response, and exposure evidence for fit and limitation.
  • Maintain an evidence ledger that records authority, version, claim, and uncertainty.
  • Produce a reproducible risk characterization and a bounded recommendation.
  • Brief a specified professional audience and defend the analysis under questioning.

Four cumulative cases

Drinking-water contamination

Problem framing, pathways, affected populations, and current requirements.

Workplace solvent use

Inhalation and dermal pathways, measurement strategy, and controls.

Extreme heat

Vulnerability, incomplete local data, and resource prioritization.

Vapor intrusion

Site evidence, model assumptions, community concerns, and alternatives.

What this sample demonstrates

  • Outcome–assessment alignment
  • Applied case architecture
  • Transparent source use
  • Multiple participation modes
  • AI-use and reproducibility expectations

This adaptable course design is not an official institutional syllabus or a record of a historical offering.

Currentness and use

Frameworks endure; controlling sources change.

Regulations, screening values, toxicity information, and agency recommendations must be checked at the point of use. The syllabus preserves the distinction among final requirements, proposed rules, guidance, recommendations, and scientific evidence.
