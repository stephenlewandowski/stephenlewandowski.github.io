The t-test on the airport ramp.

Does scenario practice change the time needed to correctly report a simulated hazard? Compare two independent groups, then ask what the result can support.

Teaching purpose

Make the reasoning visible.

The lesson starts with a measurable outcome: seconds until a correct report, with lower values indicating faster reporting. Learners distinguish variation among workers from uncertainty in the estimated difference between group means.

  1. Choose the design. Identify the worker as the unit of analysis and distinguish different workers in independent groups from repeated measurements on the same worker.
  2. Explain the uncertainty. Relate the mean difference, standard error, t statistic, two-sided p-value, and confidence interval.
  3. Defend the interpretation. Report the difference in seconds and its interval, consider practical importance, and state what the study cannot establish.

The numerical demonstration separates a larger mean gap, greater variability, and a larger planned sample. These are distinct constructed examples, not a procedure for adding observations until a result becomes significant.

A result worth discussing

The baseline has 20 workers per group: mean times of 48 and 42 seconds, each with a sample SD of 8 seconds.

The difference is 6.00 seconds, with a 95% confidence interval of 0.88 to 11.12 seconds; two-sided p ≈ .023.

If 5 seconds were a prespecified minimum worthwhile improvement, this interval would still include smaller effects. Statistical significance alone does not settle practical importance.

02 / Explore

Change one assumption. Follow the result.

The interactive Welch t-test simulation connects sample observations to the reference distribution, both tails of the test, and a 95% confidence interval.

One experiment

Adjust the true mean difference, sample size per group, and shared population SD. Draw new samples and compare the effect estimate with its uncertainty.

200 experiments

Set the true difference to zero to explore false positives. Choose a nonzero difference to estimate power. Batch percentages vary because the samples vary.

03 / Reproduce

Two designs. Two appropriate analyses.

A 25–35 minute SPSS lab carries the lesson into software, diagnostics, interpretation, and reproducibility.

Independent groups · 40 workers

04 / Interpret

A defensible conclusion includes its limits.

Ask learners to report the design, unit of analysis, difference direction, estimated effect, interval, and one limitation. A p-value is not the probability that the null hypothesis is true. A result that does not reject the null does not establish equivalence.

The paired example uses new workers; the independent groups must never be reinterpreted as matched pairs. An uncontrolled before/after comparison cannot establish that practice caused a change. None of these invented observations establishes aviation-training effectiveness or an operational benchmark.
