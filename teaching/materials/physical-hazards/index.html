Physical Hazards

A portable lesson on noise, heat, radiation, and vibration that asks learners to move from hazard recognition to measurement, control, verification, and a clear stop-or-modify-work trigger.

Open 28-page slide deckOpen student case
AudienceSenior undergraduate / introductory graduate
Default duration50–60 minutes
Source baselineChecked August 10, 2026

Instructional framework

Trace the energy. Test the evidence. Control the exposure.

The lesson uses a source–pathway–exposure–effect–measurement–control sequence that transfers across four physical agents. Learners must separate screening information from a complete exposure assessment and compliance from a fully protective professional recommendation.

Applied decision case

A fictional four-person maintenance team works near a compressor and impact tool during hot weather. Project-generated noise readings, WBGT values, work demands, acclimatization, recovery, and vibration information are sufficient for immediate protective action—but not for certifying compliance or a complete dose.

Three-sentence deliverable

  1. The evidence shows… Identify the supported exposure concerns.
  2. The evidence does not establish… Name the uncertainty that most limits the conclusion.
  3. We recommend… Specify controls, verification, and the condition for stopping or modifying work.
Lesson overviewObjectives, sequence, participation, and adaptation fields

Lesson format

The 28-page 16:9 deck pairs an original visual system with a synthetic workplace case covering noise, heat, radiation, and vibration.

The lesson is designed for adaptation across senior-undergraduate, graduate, or professional instruction.

Currentness notice

Authority distinctions remain part of the lesson.

As of August 10, 2026, federal OSHA’s heat-specific rule was still proposed; the 2026 National Emphasis Program is an enforcement directive, not a permissible exposure limit. OSHA and NIOSH noise criteria use different exchange rates. Recheck applicable requirements before delivery.
