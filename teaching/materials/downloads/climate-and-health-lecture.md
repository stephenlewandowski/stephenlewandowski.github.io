# Climate and Health

## Currentness Notice

Verified on 2026-08-09. Dataset release labels, refresh dates, and component observation periods are not interchangeable; surveillance indicators may not represent complete population incidence.

## Learning Objectives

- Classify a claim by source authority and effective status.
- Explain the topic's principal mechanisms and decision framework.
- Apply current evidence to a bounded professional scenario.
- Distinguish factual knowledge from archived instructional expression.
- Identify uncertainty, jurisdiction, and dataset limitations.
- Communicate a defensible conclusion with appropriate attribution.

## Why This Topic Matters

Climate and Health connects technical knowledge, evidence appraisal, and responsible professional judgment. This teaching resource emphasizes what current authoritative sources support, how their authority differs, and where additional review remains necessary.

## Authority and Evidence Framework

1. Identify whether the source is a regulation, proposal, recommendation, assessment, or dataset.
2. Record jurisdiction, effective status, population or geographic scope, and date semantics.
3. Apply the claim only within that scope.
4. Preserve uncertainty and schedule the next currentness review.

## Current Authoritative Baseline

- CDC's BRACE framework uses five iterative steps: anticipate impacts and assess vulnerabilities; project disease burden; assess interventions; develop and implement an adaptation plan; and evaluate impacts and improve activities. — [Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, *Building Resilience Against Climate Effects framework*](https://www.cdc.gov/climate-health/php/brace/index.html)
- CDC's climate-health resource page links the Heat & Health Tracker and Tracking Network indicators rather than serving as the underlying dataset. — [Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, *Climate and Health Program data and tools*](https://www.cdc.gov/climate-health/php/resources/data-and-tools.html)
- CDC Tracking documents historical and projected heat, heat-related emergency visits, hospitalizations, mortality, and vulnerability/preparedness indicators from multiple sources. — [Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, *Environmental Public Health Tracking heat and climate data documentation*](https://www.cdc.gov/environmental-health-tracking/php/data-research/heat-heat-related-illness.html)
- Heat-related illness surveillance may miss cases when excess heat is not explicitly documented and should not be interpreted as complete population incidence. — [Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, *Environmental Public Health Tracking heat and climate data documentation*](https://www.cdc.gov/environmental-health-tracking/php/data-research/heat-heat-related-illness.html)
- The Heat & Health Tracker uses NSSP syndromic surveillance; its primary health source is heat-related emergency-department visits reported as rates per 100,000 ED visits. — [Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, *Heat and Health Tracker source documentation*](https://www.cdc.gov/nssp/php/partnerships/cdc-heat-health-tracker-uses-nssp-data.html)
- The HHI is a national ZIP Code Tabulation Area-level index combining historical heat-health burden, sensitivity, sociodemographic, and natural/built-environment modules. — [Agency for Toxic Substances and Disease Registry, *Heat and Health Index landing page*](https://www.atsdr.cdc.gov/place-health/php/hhi/index.html)
- HHI uses ZCTAs to approximate ZIP-code geography and ranks indicators within four modules plus an overall rank. — [Agency for Toxic Substances and Disease Registry, *Heat and Health Index technical documentation*](https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/158548)
- The 2024 HHI covers the 48 contiguous states and District of Columbia; Alaska, Hawaiʻi, Puerto Rico, and other U.S. territories are excluded because required indicator coverage was insufficient. — [Agency for Toxic Substances and Disease Registry, *Heat and Health Index technical documentation*](https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/158548)
- The current registered HHI data release is labeled 2024; that release label is distinct from the various observation periods of its component indicators. — [Agency for Toxic Substances and Disease Registry, *Latest Heat and Health Index downloadable dataset and data dictionary*](https://www.atsdr.cdc.gov/place-health/php/hhi/index.html)
- WHO identifies age, health status, occupation, socioeconomic conditions, acclimatization, infrastructure, and local conditions as factors shaping heat vulnerability and impact. — [World Health Organization, *Heat and health fact sheet*](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/climate-change-heat-and-health)
- WHO/Europe's 2026 second-edition heat–health action-plan guidance is organized around eight core elements spanning governance, warning systems, vulnerable populations, communication, health-system resilience, exposure reduction, surveillance, and evaluation. — [World Health Organization Regional Office for Europe, *Heat–health action plans: guidance, second edition*](https://www.who.int/europe/publications/i/item/9789289062930)
- WHO describes climate-health pathways through extreme weather, heat, air and water quality, food systems, infectious disease, mental health, displacement, and health-system disruption. — [World Health Organization, *Climate change and health fact sheet*](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/climate-change-and-health)
- CDC/ATSDR's strategic framework emphasizes health equity, surveillance and analytics, research and evaluation, preparedness/adaptation, and education/dissemination. — [Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, *Climate and Health Strategic Framework*](https://www.cdc.gov/climate-health/php/about/strategic-framework.html)
- NCA5 Chapter 15 assesses interacting climate-health pathways including heat, air quality, infectious disease, water and food risks, mental health, and health-system effects. — [U.S. Global Change Research Program, *Fifth National Climate Assessment, Chapter 15: Human Health*](https://nca2023.globalchange.gov/chapter/15/)
- NCA5 emphasizes that exposure, sensitivity, and adaptive capacity are unevenly distributed and that climate change can worsen health inequities. — [U.S. Global Change Research Program, *Fifth National Climate Assessment, Chapter 15: Human Health*](https://nca2023.globalchange.gov/chapter/15/)
- NCA5 Chapter 30 provides region-specific evidence for Hawaiʻi and U.S.-Affiliated Pacific Islands; findings should not be generalized uniformly across islands or communities. — [U.S. Global Change Research Program, *Fifth National Climate Assessment, Chapter 30: Hawaiʻi and U.S.-Affiliated Pacific Islands*](https://nca2023.globalchange.gov/chapter/30/)

## Instructional Application

### Evidence classification

Choose three baseline statements and explain why their source types confer different kinds of authority.

### Bounded scenario

Construct a hypothetical climate and health decision. State the facts that can be applied, the assumptions that must be declared, and the questions that require additional evidence.

### Communication

Write a two-paragraph recommendation that separates current requirements, proposed changes, professional guidance, and uncertainty.

## Common Errors

- Treating a proposed rule as current final law.
- Treating technical guidance or a recommendation as automatically enforceable.
- Extending a regional, population-specific, or dataset-specific finding beyond its documented scope.
- Confusing a publication or release date with an effective date or observation period.
- Presenting archived instructional context as proof of current validity or authorship.

## Summary

- Authority and status come before application.
- Currentness is a dated claim, not a permanent property.
- Clear attribution strengthens rather than interrupts instruction.
- Public teaching resources should separate factual synthesis from private archival provenance.
