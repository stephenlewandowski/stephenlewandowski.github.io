# Drinking Water Treatment

## Currentness Notice

Current final drinking-water requirements reflect sources checked on 2026-08-09. Pending PFAS proposals are identified separately and do not establish final 2031 compliance timing. Check regulatory status before operational use.

## Learning Objectives

- Classify a claim by source authority and effective status.
- Explain the topic's principal mechanisms and decision framework.
- Apply current evidence to a bounded professional scenario.
- Identify uncertainty, jurisdiction, and dataset limitations.
- Communicate a defensible conclusion with appropriate attribution.

## Why This Topic Matters

Drinking Water Treatment connects technical knowledge, evidence appraisal, and responsible professional judgment. This teaching resource emphasizes what current authoritative sources support, how their authority differs, and where additional review remains necessary.

## Authority and Evidence Framework

1. Identify whether the source is a regulation, proposal, recommendation, assessment, or dataset.
2. Record jurisdiction, effective status, population or geographic scope, and date semantics.
3. Apply the claim only within that scope.
4. Preserve uncertainty and schedule the next currentness review.

## Current Authoritative Baseline

- WHO's fourth edition incorporating three addenda retains Water Safety Plans as a source-to-consumer risk-management approach. — [World Health Organization, *Guidelines for drinking-water quality: fourth edition incorporating the first, second and third addenda*](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240121225)
- EPA identifies national primary drinking-water regulations as legally enforceable standards for public water systems. — [U.S. Environmental Protection Agency, *National Primary Drinking Water Regulations*](https://www.epa.gov/ground-water-and-drinking-water/national-primary-drinking-water-regulations)
- The current 2024 final rule sets the PFOA maximum contaminant level at 4.0 ng/L. — [U.S. Environmental Protection Agency, *PFAS National Primary Drinking Water Regulation and implementation source*](https://www.epa.gov/sdwa/and-polyfluoroalkyl-substances-pfas)
- The current 2024 final rule sets the PFOS maximum contaminant level at 4.0 ng/L. — [U.S. Environmental Protection Agency, *PFAS National Primary Drinking Water Regulation and implementation source*](https://www.epa.gov/sdwa/and-polyfluoroalkyl-substances-pfas)
- The current final rule uses a unitless Hazard Index MCL of 1 for specified PFAS mixtures. — [U.S. Environmental Protection Agency, *PFAS National Primary Drinking Water Regulation and implementation source*](https://www.epa.gov/sdwa/and-polyfluoroalkyl-substances-pfas)
- The 2024 final rule's current implementation summary requires initial PFAS monitoring by 2027 and treatment compliance by 2029 unless a later final action changes those dates. — [U.S. Environmental Protection Agency, *PFAS National Primary Drinking Water Regulation and implementation source*](https://www.epa.gov/sdwa/and-polyfluoroalkyl-substances-pfas)
- EPA's 2026 proposal would allow qualifying systems to request an extension of PFOA/PFOS compliance to April 26, 2031; it does not itself change the final deadline. — [U.S. Environmental Protection Agency, *Proposed PFOA and PFOS Compliance Extension Rule*](https://www.epa.gov/sdwa/proposed-pfoa-and-pfos-compliance-extension-rule)
- EPA proposed rescinding the individual/Hazard Index requirements for PFHxS, PFNA, HFPO-DA, and relevant mixtures; the proposal is not current final law. — [U.S. Environmental Protection Agency, *Proposed PFAS Rescission Rule*](https://www.epa.gov/sdwa/proposed-pfas-rescission-rule)
- EPA's implementation tools are guidance supporting Lead and Copper Rule Improvements implementation; codified requirements remain controlling. — [U.S. Environmental Protection Agency, *Lead and Copper Rule implementation tools*](https://www.epa.gov/dwreginfo/lead-and-copper-rule-implementation-tools)
- EPA lists treatment technologies including activated carbon, ion exchange, membrane processes, adsorptive media, biological treatment, and ultraviolet/advanced oxidation for specified applications. — [U.S. Environmental Protection Agency, *Overview of Drinking Water Treatment Technologies*](https://www.epa.gov/sdwa/overview-drinking-water-treatment-technologies)
- SDWIS federal reporting data are submitted and frozen quarterly; submission year/quarter is version context rather than an event date. — [U.S. Environmental Protection Agency, *SDWIS Federal Reporting Services Data Dictionary*](https://www.epa.gov/DWdata/sdwis-federal-reporting-services-data-dictionary)
- ECHO's SDWA downloads derive from SDWIS and their documentation must be used to interpret compliance fields and refresh timing. — [U.S. Environmental Protection Agency, *ECHO Safe Drinking Water Act download documentation*](https://echo.epa.gov/tools/data-downloads/sdwa-download-summary)

## Instructional Application

### Evidence classification

Choose three baseline statements and explain why their source types confer different kinds of authority.

### Bounded scenario

Construct a hypothetical drinking water treatment decision. State the facts that can be applied, the assumptions that must be declared, and the questions that require additional evidence.

### Communication

Write a two-paragraph recommendation that separates current requirements, proposed changes, professional guidance, and uncertainty.

## Common Errors

- Treating a proposed rule as current final law.
- Treating technical guidance or a recommendation as automatically enforceable.
- Extending a regional, population-specific, or dataset-specific finding beyond its documented scope.
- Confusing a publication or release date with an effective date or observation period.

## Summary

- Authority and status come before application.
- Currentness is a dated claim, not a permanent property.
- Clear attribution strengthens rather than interrupts instruction.
