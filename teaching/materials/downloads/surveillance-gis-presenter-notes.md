# Presenter Notes: Surveillance and GIS for Public-Health Decisions

**Target:** 20–30 minutes. Keep the pace conversational and use slides 5 and 21 for active participation.  
**Evidence/currentness:** Official baseline checked 2026-08-10. Verify disease-specific reporting rules, current datasets/services, geographic vintages, denominators, and privacy policies before reuse. All case values and place names are synthetic.

1. **Title:** Frame the session as a decision problem, not a software demonstration.
2. **Objectives:** Emphasize professional judgment and causal restraint.
3. **Roadmap:** Preview purpose, quality, place, action.
4. **Case hook:** Ask what leadership is actually deciding.
5. **Four statements:** Give pairs 60–90 seconds; sample one sentence per category.
6. **Decision system:** Note the feedback loop and intended action.
7. **Surveillance/research:** Purpose is primary; institutional governance still controls determinations.
8. **Production history:** Ask which component could change without a disease change.
9. **Attributes:** Select attributes to match the decision; avoid a universal checklist mentality.
10. **Tradeoffs:** Invite one additional tradeoff from the room.
11. **Dataset:** State twice that it is synthetic and the suppression rule is instructional.
12. **Counts/rates:** Central and Riverbend can both be priorities for different reasons.
13. **Calculation:** Have learners audit alignment after calculating.
14. **Signal/quality:** Riverbend warrants verification, not a causal declaration.
15. **Geocoding:** Address-range coordinates can be approximations; record match quality and vintage.
16. **Geographic units:** Ask which unit best matches a service-allocation decision.
17. **Boundary/data:** A geographic file supplies shapes and identifiers, not the health measure.
18. **Classification:** Changing breaks can change visual salience; show the rule.
19. **Scale:** Connect aggregation to stability, privacy, and local usefulness.
20. **Pattern/cause:** Use ecological fallacy as the central inference limit.
21. **Privacy:** Suppressed is unavailable, not zero; do not reverse-engineer.
22. **Team decision:** Assign roles; keep deliverable to four concrete choices.
23. **Decision brief:** Invite one team to deliver the three sentences.
24. **Takeaways:** Return to evidence-to-action discipline.
25. **Sources:** Describe older CDC guidance as foundational, not newly issued; name the 2026 verification date.
