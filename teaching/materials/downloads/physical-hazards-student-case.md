# Physical Hazards Decision Case

## Scenario

A four-person maintenance team must replace a seized fastener assembly on outdoor equipment before a scheduled afternoon operation. The work occurs beside a gasoline-powered compressor and uses an impact tool. The job is physically demanding, exposed to direct sun, and expected to require repeated tool cycles.

Supervision asks whether the task can proceed as planned and what controls are needed. Your team must recommend immediate action without pretending that incomplete screening information is a full compliance assessment.

**All names and numerical values below are synthetic instructional data. They do not describe a real workplace or exposure record.**

## Available Information

| Item | Synthetic case information |
| --- | --- |
| Work window | 09:00-12:00; approximately 150 minutes of work within the three-hour window |
| Task | Six anticipated 10-minute impact-tool cycles; one worker is currently assigned all tool operation |
| Noise screening | Two A-weighted sound-level readings at the operator position: 94 and 96 dBA; compressor-area readings: 88-91 dBA |
| Noise limitations | No full-shift personal dosimetry; meter calibration record and response setting have not yet been confirmed |
| Environmental heat | On-site WBGT: 29.5 degrees C at 09:20 and 31.0 degrees C at 10:35; direct sun; light air movement |
| Work demand | Heavy during tool use and fastener handling; moderate during setup and inspection |
| Clothing/PPE | Work clothing, long sleeves, gloves, hard hat, eye protection, and hearing protectors; no impermeable ensemble |
| Recovery | Planned 10-minute break after each 50 minutes; shaded recovery area is about 80 meters away |
| Acclimatization | Two workers routinely perform hot-weather work; one returned after 10 days away; one is new to the task and local climate |
| Hydration/emergency | Potable water is available at the vehicle; radio contact is available; the written heat-emergency plan has not been reviewed with this team today |
| Vibration | Manufacturer literature lists 4.8 m/s² at the tool handle under test conditions; actual tool condition, attachment method, grip force, axes, and worker exposure are unknown |
| Schedule pressure | Equipment is needed that afternoon, but a 30-45 minute delay and task reassignment are operationally possible; equipment relocation may be possible, but feasibility is unconfirmed |

## Your Task

Prepare a three-sentence recommendation:

1. **The evidence shows...** State the most important supported exposure concerns.
2. **The evidence does not establish...** Identify the uncertainty that most limits the conclusion.
3. **We recommend...** Name immediate controls, the measurement or verification step, and a clear condition for stopping or modifying work.

## Questions

1. What physical hazards do you recognize, and where does each energy transfer begin?
2. What additional information would you seek before making a full exposure determination?
3. Which measurements matter most now? Which can wait?
4. Which uncertainty most affects today's decision?
5. Which OSHA requirement, enforcement authority, or NIOSH recommendation would you consult for each major hazard?
6. Which control should be prioritized first, and why?
7. Is PPE the first or only reasonable control here?
8. Would regulatory compliance alone satisfy your professional recommendation?
9. What observation, symptom, measurement, or plan failure would cause you to stop or modify the work?
10. How would you communicate the recommendation to workers and the supervisor without overstating certainty?

There is no single required answer. Your recommendation must be protective, feasible, explicit about uncertainty, and proportionate to the available evidence.
