# Physical Hazards in Occupational and Environmental Health

## Development Status

This adaptable lesson is designed for senior-undergraduate, graduate, or professional instruction. Host instructors can tailor its examples and emphasis to course objectives and local context.

## Intended Audience

Senior undergraduate students in Environmental and Occupational Health who have basic field knowledge but are not expected to be industrial hygienists. The intellectual level also suits an introductory graduate or professional audience.

## Default Duration

50-60 minutes. The instructor guide supplies coherent 45-, 60-, and 75-minute versions.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the lesson, students will be able to:

1. Explain how a physical hazard can be analyzed as energy or a physical condition moving from a source to a person.
2. Apply recognition, evaluation, and control reasoning to noise, heat, radiation, and vibration.
3. Interpret basic noise and heat information without confusing a screening measurement with a complete exposure assessment.
4. Distinguish a regulatory requirement, an enforcement program, a proposed rule, and a health-based professional recommendation.
5. Compare controls using the hierarchy of controls and explain why PPE is not automatically the first choice.
6. Identify consequential uncertainty and missing information in a multi-hazard assessment.
7. Develop and communicate a defensible control recommendation, verification plan, and stop-or-modify-work trigger.

## Lesson Flow

| Segment | Default time | Instructional job |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Opening multi-hazard case | 5 min | Elicit the questions professionals need before judging control adequacy |
| Physical hazards as energy transfer | 5 min | Establish source-pathway-exposure-effect-measurement-control reasoning |
| Noise case | 10-12 min | Connect level, duration, dose, measurement, authority, and controls |
| Heat case | 12-15 min | Integrate environment, work, clothing/PPE, worker, time, and emergency planning |
| Radiation and vibration contrasts | 8-10 min | Test transfer of the framework across different physical agents |
| Integrated decision case | 8-10 min | Produce and defend a bounded recommendation under uncertainty |
| Synthesis | 3-5 min | Return to hierarchy, compliance versus protection, and professional responsibility |

## Preparation

Before class, students can read the student case, underline every supplied measurement, and write two questions about information that is missing. If the host assigns an occupational-health reading, the case can be paired with that source. The package does not assume any specific Brandeis reading.

## Student Participation

Participation is evidence of reasoning rather than speaking frequency. Students first generate questions from the opening case, then compare regulatory and recommended noise criteria, critique heat information, and work in small groups on the integrated case. Contributions may be spoken, written in chat, placed on a shared board, or delivered as a short team recommendation.

## Case Strategy

The opening and integrated cases use a maintenance team working near powered equipment during hot weather. Noise and heat drive the decision; hand-arm vibration provides a contrasting third exposure. All names and values are synthetic instructional data. They do not describe a real workplace or exposure record.

The case contains enough information to propose immediate protective action but not enough to certify compliance or calculate a complete dose. More than one recommendation can be defensible when it identifies uncertainty, prioritizes effective controls, and states how performance will be verified.

## Currentness

The official-source baseline was checked on 2026-08-10. Federal OSHA's heat-specific rule remains proposed; the current 2026 heat National Emphasis Program is an enforcement directive rather than a permissible exposure limit. OSHA and NIOSH noise criteria use different exchange rates and cannot be interchanged. Applicable state-plan, institutional, task-specific, and source-specific requirements must be checked immediately before delivery or professional use.
