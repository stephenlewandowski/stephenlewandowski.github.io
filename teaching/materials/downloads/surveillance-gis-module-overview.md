# Surveillance and GIS for Public-Health Decisions

## Module purpose

This 50-minute applied module helps graduate or professional learners move from reported events to a defensible, privacy-aware geographic recommendation. It treats surveillance as a decision system and GIS as a way to link, summarize, inspect, and communicate place—not as an automatic source of causal conclusions.

## Learning objectives

By the end of the module, learners can:

1. Distinguish surveillance practice from research by purpose.
2. Evaluate a surveillance data stream using relevant system and data-quality attributes.
3. Distinguish counts from rates and select a compatible denominator.
4. Explain how geocoding, geographic unit, aggregation, and classification affect a map.
5. Interpret area-level patterns without making individual-level or causal claims.
6. Recommend a proportionate public-health action while communicating privacy limits and uncertainty.

## Preparation and sequence

- **Before class (10–15 minutes):** Read the exercise scenario, review the data dictionary, and calculate one unsuppressed area rate.
- **Opening (5 minutes):** Identify the decision and distinguish surveillance from research.
- **Surveillance reasoning (15 minutes):** Inspect timeliness, completeness, representativeness, sensitivity, and predictive value positive as decision-relevant tradeoffs.
- **Spatial reasoning (15 minutes):** Compare counts with rates; test geographic scale, denominator, and classification choices.
- **Team recommendation (10 minutes):** Draft an action recommendation with an uncertainty and privacy statement.
- **Debrief (5 minutes):** Separate observed pattern, plausible explanation, needed evidence, and defensible next action.

## Evidence and currentness

The factual baseline was checked on 2026-08-10 against official CDC and U.S. Census Bureau sources. The scenario, place names, values, classroom suppression rule, and decision prompt are newly project-generated. They are not official data and do not represent a real community. Check current reporting rules, service documentation, data vintages, and privacy policies before adapting the module for operational use.
