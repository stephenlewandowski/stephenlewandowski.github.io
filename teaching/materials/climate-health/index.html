Climate and Health

A teaching resource on climate-health pathways, uneven vulnerability, adaptation, surveillance limitations, and the date semantics required to interpret changing datasets responsibly.

Open technical reportOpen lecture notes
Decision frameEvidence → vulnerability → adaptation
Data focusHeat surveillance and composite indices
Verified throughAugust 9, 2026

Instructional design

Release labels, refresh dates, and observation periods are different facts.

Learners connect heat, air and water quality, infectious disease, food systems, mental health, displacement, and health-system disruption to populations whose exposure, sensitivity, and adaptive capacity differ.

Evidence in use

  • The CDC BRACE framework as an iterative adaptation process.
  • Surveillance indicators that do not represent complete population incidence.
  • Heat & Health Tracker rates and their denominator.
  • The Heat and Health Index, its modules, geography, and coverage limits.
  • National and regional assessment evidence that should not be generalized beyond scope.

Application sequence

  1. Classify assessment, guidance, and dataset statements by authority and scope.
  2. Frame a bounded climate-health decision and identify the missing local evidence.
  3. Recommend an adaptation action while stating the surveillance, geography, and equity limits.

What this sample demonstrates

  • Climate-health pathway synthesis
  • Vulnerability as more than exposure
  • Dataset-period discipline
  • Regional and jurisdictional restraint
  • Adaptation under uncertainty

The resource does not treat a composite index or surveillance indicator as a complete measure of individual risk or population incidence.

Currentness notice

Dataset dates must remain attached to their meaning.

The baseline was verified on August 9, 2026. Before reuse, verify data releases, component observation periods, service documentation, and the geographic and population coverage of each indicator.
