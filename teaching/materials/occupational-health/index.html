Occupational Health and Industrial Hygiene

A paired lecture and workplace exercise that turns regulations, recommendations, sampling evidence, and pending rulemaking into a transparent exposure-control plan.

SettingIndoor fabrication + outdoor maintenance

Instructional design

Compliance values are one part of a professional judgment.

Learners distinguish OSHA requirements from NIOSH recommendations, design a defensible sampling strategy, and select controls that match the strength and limits of the evidence.

The exercise asks learners to produce

  • An authority map linking each claim to issuer, status, and operational implication.
  • A representative and worst-case exposure-assessment plan.
  • A control sequence using elimination, substitution, engineering, administrative, and PPE options.
  • A heat plan that keeps current enforcement authorities separate from proposed rulemaking.
  • A concise management memo that states what is known, uncertain, and required next.

Assessment emphasis

The work is assessed for authority classification, sampling rationale, hierarchy-of-controls reasoning, treatment of uncertainty, and whether the recommendation is feasible without overstating what the available evidence proves.

What this sample demonstrates

  • Regulation–recommendation distinctions
  • Sampling as a decision design problem
  • Controls before default reliance on PPE
  • Integration of heat and airborne hazards
  • Professional memo writing

The fictional employer keeps the exercise focused on exposure assessment without implying a determination about a real workplace.

Currentness notice

The federal heat-specific standard remains proposed.

The baseline was verified on August 9, 2026. Existing OSHA enforcement authorities and the 2026 National Emphasis Program are treated separately from the proposed rule; applicable jurisdiction and current status should be rechecked before use.
